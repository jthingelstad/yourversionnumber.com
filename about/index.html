About

Where this came from, how the math works, and what happens to your data. (Nothing happens to your data.)

Where it came from

In February 2018 Jamie Thingelstad wrote a post called “Your Version Number”, borrowing an idea from his friend David Hussman: track your age the way software tracks releases. He was on 4.6.52 at the time. This site is that post, made clickable.

The birthday math

Take your age in whole years. MAJOR is that age divided by ten and rounded down — your decade. MINOR is the remainder — your year inside that decade. PATCH is the number of days since your most recent birthday.

So someone aged 52 years and 113 days is 5.2.113. The day they turn 53, they ship 5.3.0 — a minor bump, backwards-compatible. At 60 it’s 6.0.0, a major release.

February 29 birthdays get March 1 as their anniversary in non-leap years. Dates in the future are skipped rather than counted backwards.

The work math

The Work Edition anchors on a start date instead of a birthday. YEARS is completed years of tenure. QUARTERS is which quarter of your current tenure year you’re in, counted in three-month steps from your start date rather than from January — start on February 10 and your Q1 begins May 10. DAYS counts business days into that quarter, so weekends don’t move the number.

Your dates stay yours

This is a static site. There is no server to send anything to, no database, no account, and no cookie. Every birthday and start date lives in the URL in your address bar and nowhere else. Close the tab and it is gone; bookmark it and it comes back.

Visit counts go to Tinylytics, a privacy-friendly analytics service. Before any of that leaves your browser, both the page URL and the referrer are trimmed down to origin and path, so the query string carrying your dates is never transmitted. The one thing kept in local storage is a single true/false flag recording whether you have seen this page before.

The flip side of putting everything in the URL: anyone you send a link to can read the dates in it, and so can your browser’s history sync. It is a fine place for birthdays you’d put on a card. It is not a place for anything sensitive.

Themes

There are 42 themes across the two editions, and the gallery shows all of them. Each one is a single standalone CSS file with no JavaScript — a deliberate restriction, because a theme that could run code could also read the birthdays in the URL and phone them home.

New themes are genuinely welcome. Add a stylesheet, add one entry to the theme list, open a pull request: CONTRIBUTING.md has the details.
