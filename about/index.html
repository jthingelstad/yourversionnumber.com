About

Where this came from, how the arithmetic works, and exactly what is stored. (Almost nothing.)

Origin

In February 2018 Jamie Thingelstad wrote “Your Version Number”, borrowing an idea from his friend David Hussman: track your age the way software tracks releases. He was on 4.6.52 at the time. This site is that post, made clickable.

The day they turn 53 they ship 5.3.0 — a minor bump, backwards-compatible. At 60 it is 6.0.0, a major release. February 29 birthdays get March 1 in non-leap years, and future dates are skipped rather than counted backwards.

The work arithmetic

The Work Edition anchors on a start date. YEARS is completed years of tenure. QUARTERS is which quarter of the current tenure year you are in, counted in three-month steps from the start date rather than from January — start on February 10 and Q1 begins May 10. DAYS counts business days, so weekends do not move it.

What a card stores

Stored per card400 DAYS

Sending a card is the only thing on this site that writes anything down. A card record holds a name, a date, your note, and the name you signed it with. Nothing else: no email address, no account, no IP address, and no analytics on the card page at all. Every record expires automatically 400 days after it is made.

Entering a date on either edition stores nothing. That flow has no server behind it and never has — the dates live in your address bar and nowhere else.

Cards carry a noindex header, so they never enter search. A card cannot be edited after it is sent, which keeps the record trivial and the link trustworthy.

Your dates stay yours

Visit counts go to Tinylytics. Before anything leaves your browser, both the page URL and the referrer are trimmed to origin and path, so the query string carrying your dates is never transmitted. One boolean in local storage records whether you have seen the intro.

The flip side of putting everything in the URL: anyone you send a link to can read the dates in it, and so can your browser’s history sync. Fine for birthdays you would put on a card. Not a place for anything sensitive.

Themes

Twenty-eight themes, each one a single standalone stylesheet with no JavaScript — a deliberate restriction, because a theme that could run code could also read the dates in the URL and send them somewhere. Anything a theme needs comes from seven generic hooks in the core. Browse them in the gallery, or see them carrying real people.
