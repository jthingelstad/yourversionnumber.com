How the number is calculated

Four figures, one start date, no discretion applied.

Yearscompleted years in the seat
Quarters1 to 4, in three-month steps from your anniversary
Daysbusiness days into the current quarter; a quarter starts at zero
Buildevery business day ever logged — never resets

Someone three years and change into a job is 3.2.40+888: three completed years, in the second quarter of the fourth, forty business days into that quarter, and 888 business days in the seat altogether. The build goes after the + because that is where build metadata goes in a real version string, and it goes up forever whether or not anything improved.

Why the build number

A birthday resets. Tenure accrues. Nobody arrives on their work anniversary with a clean slate; the whole feeling of tenure is accumulation, and a purely cyclical triple cannot express it. Nobody knows what build they are on, everybody has seen one in an About dialog, and here you still are.

Why quarters start on your anniversary

Because it is your number, not the company’s. Anchoring to a calendar or fiscal quarter would mean two people with identical tenure showing different figures, which defeats the purpose. Quarter 1 begins on the day you started; quarter 4 ends the day before your next anniversary. If an anniversary lands past the end of a month it rolls forward: three months after 31 January is 1 May.

Why weekends do not count

Monday to Friday only, with no holiday calendar, because no holiday calendar could be correct for everyone. Saturday and Sunday produce no change. Neither does annual leave, which the site has no way of knowing about and would not record if it did.

Records

None are kept. The start date is read from the address bar and nowhere else — names, dates, the format, and on a notice the remarks and who submitted it. There is no account, no database and nothing to request under any policy. This is not a benefit.
